## 1. The read

ProductionX is not short of capability, credibility or craft. It is short of
**demand it controls** and **proof it can point at**.

### What is genuinely strong

**The founder's position is unusual and hard to copy.** Three years brand-side —
Content Producer at Mercedes-Benz across AP & Telangana, Head of Creative &
Marketing at Ujwala Group — is the difference between a studio that invoices
brands and one that has sat in the brand's chair. Content lead on India's first
Mercedes-Maybach showroom is a sentence very few studios in the country can write.
The site says this. It does not yet *sell* on it.

**The three-pillar stack is real, not a services list.** Strategy, production and
digital under one roof, with an in-house crew and an edit suite, removes the
translation tax that every multi-supplier client pays. That is a true operating
advantage.

**The previz capability is the most valuable thing in the building** and is
currently presented as the fourth item in the third pillar.

**The infrastructure is ahead of the marketing.** Locked palette, generated logo
matrix, print-ready card, verified profile copy, FAQ schema, `llms.txt`, sitemap.
Most studios at this stage have none of it. The house is built; nobody has been
invited in.

### What is actually blocking growth

**1. Capability proof, not outcome proof.** The work section shows verticals,
rosters and craft — deliberately, and honestly, with no invented project titles.
But a marketing head signing a ₹1L/month retainer is not buying frames, they are
buying a result. There is not one published number that belongs to a client:
no view counts, no enquiry lift, no units sold, no cost-per-lead. This is the
single biggest conversion blocker on the site and in every pitch.

**2. No price anchor anywhere.** Pricing was dropped from the site (commit
`9b7acb0`). That protects negotiating room, but it costs more than it saves: with
no number, the enquiry form fills with people who were never going to spend
₹2L, and the founder's calendar — the scarcest resource in the business — pays
for the filtering. A "from ₹" anchor disqualifies the wrong buyer before they
book a call, and signals tier to the right one.

**3. Every channel is passive.** Site, WhatsApp, referral. There is no engine
that produces a qualified conversation on a Tuesday when nobody happened to ask
around. Revenue is therefore a function of who remembered ProductionX this month.

**4. The positioning is horizontal in a market that rewards vertical.** "Three
disciplines under one roof" is a superb *retention* argument — it is why a client
consolidates onto one retainer. It is a weak *acquisition* argument, because a
developer looking for a walkthrough and a dealership looking for launch films are
both searching for a specialist, and "we do everything" reads as "we specialise in
nothing" to a stranger.

**5. Revenue is lumpy, and the ladder from project to retainer is not built.**
The terms are right — three-month minimum, 50% advance. But nothing in the funnel
is designed to convert a one-off film buyer into a retained account, which is the
only path to a business that does not restart every January.

**6. The founder is the product.** All credibility, all sales, most creative
direction. That is normal at this stage and dangerous past it. It caps revenue at
the founder's available hours and makes the business unsellable.

## 2. The strategic call

> **Keep the three-pillar studio as the brand. Go to market with two spears.**

The studio identity stays exactly as it is — it is well-built and it is what makes
clients consolidate. But acquisition stops being "we do brand, content and
digital for good brands in Hyderabad" and becomes two specific, nameable
propositions aimed at two specific buyers.

### Spear A — Real-estate pre-launch _(the growth spear)_

**Proposition:** *Sell the building before it exists.*

Previsualisation, launch microsite, brochure and ad frames, campaign and social —
one studio, from first render to first booking.

Why this is the right bet:

- **Almost nobody local is positioned here.** Architectural-viz vendors render
  buildings; marketing agencies run campaigns. Very few do both, and the seam
  between them is where developers lose months. ProductionX already sits on both
  sides — and has shipped the microsite half (Sattva Amora).
- **The buyer has urgent budget.** Pre-launch sales fund construction. Anything
  that pulls bookings forward is not a marketing cost to a developer, it is
  working capital.
- **It pulls the whole studio through.** One previz sale opens the microsite, the
  ad films, the social management and the paid budget. The largest average
  contract value available to this business.
- **It produces the case study that fixes blocker #1.** Units booked in pre-launch
  is a number a client will let you publish, because it flatters them too.
- **Margin is structurally better.** Render pipeline and web build carry no crew
  day-rate, no equipment, no travel.

### Spear B — Premium automotive & luxury retail _(the credibility spear)_

**Proposition:** *We ran content inside Mercedes-Benz. Now we run it for you.*

Launch films, showroom and delivery content, always-on social for dealerships,
jewellery, premium fashion and lifestyle retail.

Why: the credibility is already banked and unarguable — Mercedes-Benz, the Maybach
showroom, Silver Star, Krishna Motors, Tanishq, 1UJ. This is the fastest close in
the business because the objection ("do you understand our category?") is answered
before it is raised. Dealer groups also have a structural advantage: **one sale,
many outlets, recurring monthly need.**

### Everything else becomes fill, not focus

Hospitality, F&B, corporate, events, weddings. Accept the good ones — they pay
crew salaries between spear projects, and the Hole in the Wall style "one morning
of coverage, a month of content" model is genuinely excellent. But no outbound
effort, no paid budget and no new case-study investment goes here. Fill work is
harvested; spear work is hunted.

## 3. Offer architecture

Four rungs. Every rung is designed to end with the client one rung further up.

### Rung 1 — Content Day _(the trojan horse)_

One shoot day. Thirty days of content: reels, stills, stories, cutdowns.
Fixed price, fixed scope, no strategy phase, delivered inside two weeks.

- **Anchor: ₹55,000 [verify]**
- **Why it exists:** it converts "send me a quote" into a signed, cheap, low-risk
  first transaction. It is not a discount — it is a paid trial of the machine.
- **The move:** the delivery deck ends with the month-two calendar already built.
  ~40% [verify] should step up to Rung 2 within sixty days. Track this number;
  it is the health metric for the whole ladder.

### Rung 2 — Always-On _(the core business)_

Monthly retainer: content calendar, shoot cadence, edit, captions, scheduling,
community, competitor tracking, monthly analytics, paid integration.

- **Essential ₹75,000/mo · Growth ₹1,25,000/mo · Premium ₹2,00,000/mo+ [verify]**
- Three-month minimum, 50% advance — the terms already published.
- **This is the number that matters.** Retainer revenue is the only revenue that
  makes the business plannable, hireable and worth something to a buyer.

### Rung 3 — Project spikes

Brand film (₹2.5–8L), ad film, website build (₹1.5–5L), campaign launch. **[verify]**

Sold standalone, but never quoted standalone: every project proposal carries a
"what happens after delivery" page pricing Rung 2. A film without a distribution
plan is a beautiful file on a hard drive, and saying so in the proposal is both
true and the most natural upsell in the business.

### Rung 4 — Pre-Launch System _(the flagship)_

The Spear A offer, packaged: previz walkthroughs and hero frames · launch
microsite with enquiry capture · brochure and collateral frames · launch film ·
paid campaign · social management through the booking window.

- **₹6–15L over four to six months [verify]**
- Sold to the developer's marketing or sales head, not the procurement desk.
- Priced against inventory value, never against day rate. A studio that quotes
  ₹8L against a ₹200 Cr project is not expensive; it is rounding error against
  the cost of a slow pre-launch — and that is exactly how the conversation should
  be framed.

### The pricing decision

Publish "from" numbers on the site. Not rate cards — anchors:

> Content Day from ₹55,000 · Always-On from ₹75,000/month · Brand films from
> ₹2,50,000 · Websites from ₹1,50,000 · Pre-launch systems quoted per project

This costs nothing in negotiating room (every number is a floor) and buys back
the founder's calendar. **[verify against the last ten closed deals — if the real
floors are higher, publish the real ones.]**

## 4. The demand engine

Ranked by return per rupee and per founder-hour, for this business specifically.

### 4.1 Founder-led publishing — start here, this week

**The single highest-ROI channel available, and it is currently unused.**

Kiran's brand-side story is the studio's whole differentiator, and it is sitting
in an About section where it reaches only people who already found the site. On
LinkedIn it reaches marketing heads directly — who are precisely the buyer.

- **LinkedIn, 3×/week.** Not "we delivered a film for X." Write the thing only a
  brand-side person can write: what a brand manager actually gets judged on, why
  agency decks fail internally, what the Maybach launch taught about content that
  sells versus content that wins awards, how to brief a studio so you get what
  you wanted.
- **Instagram, daily-ish.** The work, the set, the process. Reels are the studio's
  own product — the grid is the portfolio and the ad at once.
- **One long-form piece a month** on the site, which then feeds a month of posts.

Cost: time. Expected: the first inbound from this channel inside 6–8 weeks, and
compounding from there. This is also the founder-dependency hedge — a personal
brand is the asset that survives every other channel failing.

### 4.2 Own local search — and eat the cooking

ProductionX sells local SEO. It should visibly rank for its own terms, because
that is the proof.

- **Google Business Profile is the priority.** It feeds Maps and the local pack,
  which is where "ad film makers near me" actually lands. `brand/social-profiles.md`
  already has the compliant copy written — publish it, list every service
  separately, post work weekly, and ask every delivered client for a review.
  **Target: 25+ reviews in six months.**
- **Target terms:** `ad film makers in Hyderabad`, `brand film production
  Hyderabad`, `real estate walkthrough video Hyderabad`, `3D previsualisation real
  estate India`, `social media management Hyderabad`, `corporate video Hyderabad`.
- **One landing page per spear**, not one page for everything: `/real-estate` and
  `/automotive`, each with its own proof, its own FAQ and its own form. The home
  page can stay a studio statement; the spear pages do the ranking and the
  converting.
- **Answer engines.** `llms.txt` and FAQ schema are already in place — extend the
  FAQ to cover the questions a developer or dealer asks ("what does real-estate
  previz cost", "how long does a pre-launch campaign take"). When a buyer asks an
  assistant for a studio in Hyderabad, the studio with structured, quotable
  answers is the one that gets named.

### 4.3 Named-account outbound — where the big tickets are

Inbound will not deliver a ₹10L developer engagement in the next six months.
Outbound will.

- **Build one list of 100 accounts:** 60 Hyderabad residential developers with a
  project launching in the next 12 months, 40 premium auto dealer groups and
  luxury retail chains across AP & Telangana. Name the marketing head or sales
  head, not the info@ address.
- **Build the door-opener before the list.** Pick one real, recognisable, unbuilt
  Hyderabad project and produce a 45-second previz sequence on spec. That single
  asset is worth more than any deck: it converts "we do previz" into "here is
  your building, moving." Nothing else in this plan opens a ₹10L conversation
  as cheaply.
- **Sequence:** LinkedIn connect → the spec reel with one line of context → a
  short email two days later → one follow-up → stop. Fifteen accounts a week is
  enough; this is a precision channel, not a volume one.
- **Expect 2–4 real conversations per 100 touched [verify].** At this ticket size,
  one close pays for the quarter.

### 4.4 Formalise referral and partners

Referral is already the studio's main channel; it is simply unmanaged.

- **Architects, interior designers and real-estate brokers** sit next to Spear A
  buyers and are asked "who films this?" constantly. A stated 10% referral fee
  and a quarterly check-in turns goodwill into pipeline.
- **PR firms, event companies and wedding planners** for fill work.
- **The UK agency partnership is an under-used asset.** International production
  partner is a strong line — and India-cost production for UK-priced clients is a
  high-margin lane worth deliberately expanding rather than accepting when it
  arrives.
- **Ask every satisfied client, once, at delivery.** Not "keep us in mind" —
  "who else is launching something this quarter?"

### 4.5 Paid — deliberately last

Do not run acquisition ads until there is one case study with a number in it.
Paid amplifies a message; there is not yet a message with proof behind it, and
spending against capability claims is how studios burn ₹50k learning nothing.

**Then, in this order:** retargeting site visitors (cheapest, warmest) → Meta
video ads to lookalikes of the enquiry list → Google Search on the four
highest-intent local terms. Start at ₹30–40k/month and only scale a channel that
has produced two closed deals.

## 5. Proof — the highest-priority fix

Nothing in this plan works as well without this, and it costs almost nothing.

**Three case studies, this quarter.** Pick the three delivered projects with the
most cooperative clients and build:

- The brief and the constraint
- What was made
- **One number** — views, enquiries, footfall, bookings, cost-per-lead, units sold
- The client saying one sentence in their own words

Get permission properly, offer the client the content back for their own channels
as the trade, and accept a blinded version ("a Hyderabad dealer group") if a name
is off-limits. A blinded number beats a named adjective every time.

**Then instrument everything going forward.** From the next engagement onward,
agree the measured outcome *in the proposal*, before the work starts. A studio
that opens with "here is how we will know this worked" is already a different
category of supplier — and it makes every future case study automatic rather than
archaeological.

## 6. The business, not just the marketing

**Cash.** The 50%-advance, three-month-minimum terms are right. Add: e-mandate or
standing instruction for retainers so collection is not a monthly conversation, a
stated late-payment term, and an annual pre-pay option at ~8% discount for the
retainers that can take it. Cash timing kills more studios than pricing does.

**Capacity and the first hire.** The founder is currently strategy, sales,
creative direction and quality control. The first hire should be a **producer /
account lead** who owns delivery scheduling and client comms — bought at roughly
₹6L/month recurring revenue [verify], because that is the role that returns the
founder's hours to sales and creative, which is where his margin actually is. An
in-house editor is the second hire; keep the shooting crew flexible on a freelance
bench.

**Mix, and where the margin really is.** Crew-based production is capacity-capped
and margin-capped: more revenue needs more days, more people, more kit. Previz,
web and social management are not — they scale on process and tooling. The
twelve-month mix should move deliberately toward those lines. That is the
difference between a studio that grows and a studio that just gets busier.

**Founder dependency.** Documented process (the five steps are already public),
a producer who can run discovery, and case studies that credit the studio rather
than the founder. The personal brand in §4.1 is not in tension with this — it
builds the pipeline while the delivery system learns to run without him.

**An illustrative twelve-month shape [verify against real numbers]:**

| | Now (assumed) | Month 12 target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly revenue | ₹5L | ₹12.5L |
| Retainer share | ~20% | **60%+** |
| Retained accounts | 2–3 | 8 |
| Avg contract value | ₹1.5L | ₹4L |
| Pre-launch systems sold | 0 | 2 |

The retainer share is the line that matters. Revenue can be hit with one lucky
project; 60% recurring is what makes the business plannable, hireable and worth
something.

## 8. The twelve-month arc

- **Q1 — Proof and position.** Case studies, spear pages, pricing anchors, founder
  publishing started, outbound machine built.
- **Q2 — Land the flagship.** First Pre-Launch System closed and delivered. First
  producer hire. Retainers to 5.
- **Q3 — Systematise.** Pre-launch productised into a repeatable offer with a
  named process. Paid scaled against proven creative. Retainers to 7.
- **Q4 — Category.** Two pre-launch systems delivered with published numbers.
  ProductionX known in Hyderabad as *the* pre-launch studio, not *a* video
  studio. Retainers to 8, 60% recurring, pre-sell Q1.

## 9. What to measure

**Weekly:** enquiries by source · qualified briefs · proposals out · LinkedIn
impressions and profile views · outbound touches.

**Monthly:** revenue split project vs retainer · retained accounts · average
contract value · close rate by source · cost per enquiry by channel · Google
Business Profile calls and direction requests · Content Day → retainer conversion.

**Quarterly:** revenue per full-time person · retainer share of total · gross
margin by line (production vs previz vs web vs social) · client concentration —
**no single client above 30% of revenue** · pipeline value by spear.

**The one number to watch:** *retainer revenue as a share of total.* Every
decision in this document is ultimately in service of moving it from ~20% to 60%.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Founder bottleneck** | Caps revenue at his hours; makes the business unsellable | Producer hire at ₹6L/mo; documented process; case studies credited to the studio |
| **Client concentration** | One roster name leaving takes a quarter with it | Cap any client at 30% of revenue; keep the ladder feeding new logos |
| **Previz bet does not land** | Spear A is the growth thesis | Test with the spec reel *before* investing further — cheap, fast falsification |
| **AI commoditises content** | "We can shoot it" is worth less every year | Lean into it: the studio already sells AI as a line. The defensible layer is strategy and outcome, not the shutter |
| **Lumpy cash** | Project-led revenue starves the studio between wins | Retainer share to 60%; advances and mandates; annual pre-pay |
| **Discounting under pressure** | Erodes the premium the whole brand rests on | Published floors; scope down, never price down |
