## LinkedIn — 3 posts

### Post 1 — Monday. When we use a real crew vs. when we generate.

> Both, chosen on what the job needs — never AI because it's cheaper, never
> a full crew because it's traditional.
>
> Real crew: brand films, product and fashion shoots, event coverage. When
> there's something to physically point a camera at, we shoot it.
>
> Generated: unbuilt property, concept frames, campaign cutaways for brands
> with no budget for a full production unit. When a shoot is genuinely
> impractical, not just expensive.
>
> The judgment call is the actual skill. Either tool used on the wrong job
> produces worse work, not just different work.
>
> — ProductionX, Hyderabad

### Post 2 — Wednesday. What a zero-shoot-budget brand can still get.
> A brand with no budget for a full production unit isn't out of options
> anymore — product scenes, concept frames and campaign cutaways can be
> generated instead of filmed.
>
> This isn't a lesser version of "real" production. It's a different tool for
> a specific problem: no product yet, no location, no budget for a crew day.
> Used well, a viewer can't tell — and used badly, everyone can. That gap is
> where a producer's judgment still matters more than the tool.
>
> — ProductionX, Hyderabad

### Post 3 — Friday. Why AI content still needs a producer, not just a prompt.
> The frames that get used are never the first ones generated. There's a
> reshoot-equivalent step — refining, correcting, rejecting — that a prompt
> alone doesn't do.
>
> The tool changed what's possible. It didn't remove the judgment call about
> what's actually good enough to put a brand's name on.
>
> — ProductionX, Hyderabad
