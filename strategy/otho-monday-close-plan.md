# OTHO Realty — Monday 24 Aug, founder meeting

The single most important hour in the business right now. One month of meetings,
a strategy presentation, a website prototype and ongoing "initial support" have
all been given away free. Monday is where that either converts or it doesn't.

**Objective: leave the room with a signature and an advance. Not another meeting.**

---

## Read the situation honestly

**What's good:** they came to you, they liked the strategy, they liked the
prototype, and they want end-to-end. That is a genuinely strong position.

**What's dangerous:**

1. **A month of free work has removed their urgency.** They already have your
   thinking and your prototype. Every week that passes, the value of what you've
   given decays and their need to sign decreases. Reciprocity is at its peak
   right now and falls from here.
2. **One deal is about to become 75% of your revenue.** If OTHO is ₹1.5L/mo
   against a ₹2L base, you have not built a business — you've taken a job with
   no contract and no notice period.
3. **You may be badly underpriced.** See the warning below. Read it before you
   name a number.

---

## ⚠️ The ₹2L website is probably underpriced — read this first

"Carwale for real estate" is not a website. It is a **marketplace platform**:
property listings, search and filtering, image handling at scale, enquiry
capture, agent or builder dashboards, an admin back office, and a database that
has to stay accurate as inventory changes daily.

Front-end plus back-end for that is realistically **₹4–8L of work**, even built
fast with AI assistance. At ₹2L you are not winning a deal — you are buying
months of unpaid scope creep at the exact moment you cannot afford it. And once
₹2L is the anchor, every future phase gets compared to it.

**The fix is not to raise the price on Monday. It is to shrink what ₹2L buys.**

> **Phase 1 — ₹2,00,000 — "Launch Platform" (6 weeks)**
> Listings, search and filters, enquiry capture, admin panel to add and edit
> properties, mobile-responsive, live on their domain.
>
> **Phase 2 — quoted separately** — user accounts, saved searches, builder
> dashboards, comparison tools, analytics, integrations.

Same ₹2L. Same signature Monday. But the boundary is written down, so Phase 2 is
a new invoice instead of a free favour. **Put that Phase 1 / Phase 2 line in
writing before you leave the room** — it is the difference between a ₹2L project
and a ₹2L project that eats four months.

---

## The one tactical move that matters most

**Split the website from the retainer. Sign the website Monday.**

Right now both decisions are bundled, so the small, easy, cash-generating one
(₹2L website, clear scope, you deliver it yourself) is being held hostage by the
big, slow, scary one (₹1.5L/mo forever, unclear scope, needs their whole
organisation to commit).

> "The website is a defined project with a defined price — we can start that
> immediately. The marketing partnership is a bigger decision and I don't expect
> you to make both today. Let's lock the platform and let the marketing scope
> follow it."

This is easier for them to say yes to, and it gets you **₹1,00,000 in advance
this week** instead of ₹0 while a ₹3.5L decision drifts through another month.

If you get only one thing on Monday, get this.

---

## Pricing to put on the table

**Website — ₹2,00,000**
- 50% advance on signing: **₹1,00,000**
- 30% at prototype approval: ₹60,000
- 20% at launch: ₹40,000

**Marketing retainer — ₹1,50,000/month**, starting at platform launch
- Payable monthly in advance
- 6-month initial term
- Scope fixed in a one-page annexure — attached to the agreement, not agreed verbally

**Ad spend is separate and paid by OTHO directly.** Say this explicitly. If ad
budget comes out of your ₹1.5L, your real fee is a fraction of it and the
retainer stops being viable the moment they want to scale spend.

---

## Handling the free work — turn it into leverage, not resentment

Do not complain about it. Convert it. Early in the meeting:

> "Over the last month I've put together the marketing strategy, the platform
> prototype and the positioning work — that's roughly ₹1.5L of work, and I did it
> because I wanted you to see how we think rather than just read a proposal.
> I'm glad it landed. What I'd like today is to put a structure around it so we
> can actually build it."

This does three things at once: prices the free work so they feel its value,
explains *why* you did it so you look generous rather than desperate, and moves
straight to structure. It also quietly closes the free-work era — everything
after Monday is scoped and billed.

---

## Meeting structure — 45 minutes

| Min | What | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Rapport, then the value-already-delivered line above | Anchor reciprocity |
| 5–15 | Founder talks: their targets, timeline, what success looks like in 12 months | Let them sell themselves; you learn the real budget |
| 15–25 | Walk the prototype, tie every screen to a business outcome | Make it concrete |
| 25–35 | Phase 1 / Phase 2 split + pricing + terms | The ask |
| 35–45 | **Close and date it** | The point of the meeting |

---

## The close — rehearse this until it's natural

> "Here's what I'd suggest. We sign the platform today, I invoice the advance,
> and we start Wednesday — you'd be live in six weeks. The marketing side we
> scope over the next two weeks so it's ready to switch on the day the platform
> launches. Does that work?"

Then **stop talking.** Whoever speaks first after the ask, loses. Let the silence
sit however long it sits.

---

## Objection handling

**"Send us the proposal, we'll review."**
> "I'll send it today. Can we put fifteen minutes in the diary for Wednesday to
> go through it? I'd rather answer questions live than have it sit in an inbox."
*Never leave without the next date in the calendar.*

**"₹2L feels high for a website."**
> "It would be, for a website. This is a listings platform with a back office —
> different animal. What I can do is stage it: Phase 1 gets you live and taking
> enquiries at ₹2L, and we quote the rest once you're running."
*Never drop the price. Change what's included.*

**"Can we start marketing before the platform's ready?"**
Yes — and take the money: ₹75k/month interim for brand and content groundwork,
adjusted into the full retainer at launch. Cash now, and it makes the retainer
feel like a continuation rather than a new decision.

**"We need to think about the monthly commitment."**
> "Completely fair — that's why I'd separate them. Sign the platform now, decide
> the retainer when you can see it working."
*This objection is exactly why you split them.*

**"Can you reduce the retainer?"**
> "I can reduce the scope. ₹1.5L covers the full operation. If you want to start
> narrower, ₹90k covers content and social while you build up." 
*Scope down, never rate down.*

---

## What you must not do

- **Do not do more free work to win the deal.** You are past the point where more
  giving helps. It now reads as need, and need loses negotiations.
- **Do not agree to "end-to-end everything"** without a written scope annexure.
  "End to end" is how one client becomes three clients' worth of work at one
  client's price.
- **Do not walk out with only a verbal yes.** Verbal yes plus no advance equals
  another month of meetings.
- **Do not let the retainer decision block the website signature.**

---

## Take these into the room

- Printed one-page proposal — Phase 1 scope, price, payment schedule, dates
- Simple agreement they can sign on the spot
- Prototype on a laptop, loaded and working before you walk in
- Bank details for the advance invoice
- The scope annexure for the retainer, even in draft

Sign-on-the-spot is worth more than a beautiful PDF emailed at 9pm.

---

## After Monday, whatever happens

**If it closes:** OTHO becomes 75% of revenue. That is a risk, not a victory.
Designerz Hub and two more accounts still matter — keep selling in weeks two and
three, when the temptation to relax will be strongest.

**If it doesn't close Monday:** get the reason and the date. "Not yet" is
survivable; "no answer" is not. If there's no advance by 31 Aug, OTHO stops being
a plan and becomes a maybe — and everything shifts to Designerz Hub, the ₹25k
completion, and warm-network outreach.
